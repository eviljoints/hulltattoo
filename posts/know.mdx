## 1. A portfolio matters more than a trend

Compare several examples of an artist’s work in the style you want. Ask about scale, placement and how much detail will work in the available space. A design that looks striking on a screen may need adapting for skin.

Explore [Mike’s realism and cover-ups](/mike/), [Harley’s dotwork](/harley/) and [Jen’s colour neo traditional pieces](/jen/) to see the differences for yourself.

## 2. You can set personal boundaries

You do not have to let anyone touch a new tattoo to admire it. Follow the [aftercare instructions](/aftercare/) for your tattoo and tell your artist if you have concerns about privacy or positioning during the appointment.

## 3. The whole budget needs planning

Discuss the likely appointment length before booking. Read the price and payment terms at checkout, and ask what happens if the design needs more than one session. Leave room in your plans for the aftercare routine and any follow-up the artist recommends.

## 4. You do not have to rush a design

A popular image may be a useful reference, but choose the parts you actually like: shape, subject, colour or contrast. Bring those references to the artist so the design can be developed for your placement rather than copied without thought.

## 5. Good questions make a better starting point

Before choosing a date, write down the idea, approximate size, placement and any existing ink nearby. Send clear images with your enquiry and ask how much time to reserve. If an answer is unclear, ask again before paying.
